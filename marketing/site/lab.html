The Strategy Lab

From a hunch to a verdict, in one workspace.

The Strategy Lab is where an idea becomes a tested strategy. Define it, run it against point-in-time history, and read an out-of-sample verdict that won't flatter you — all without leaving the page.

The honest workflow

Five steps. No shortcuts to a clean equity curve.

The Lab puts the whole research loop in front of you, in the order that keeps you honest — the easy in-sample win is never the last word.

  • Pick a universe — choose the assets and the window, with survivorship-aware membership baked in.
  • Choose a factor — wire up your signal from the library or your own logic, fully editable.
  • Run a point-in-time backtest — every bar sees only what was knowable then, never tomorrow's data.
  • Read the out-of-sample verdict — held-out and walk-forward results decide whether it survives.
  • Ship a shareable report — one link, methodology attached, nothing hidden behind it.
Inside the Lab

Out-of-sample first

The verdict reads the data you didn't get to tune.

In-sample, almost anything looks like alpha. The Lab refuses to lead with it. The verdict card states the call against held-out and walk-forward returns, then shows the deflated Sharpe — discounted for how many variants you tried — right next to it.

  • Survives / doesn't survive, stated plainly at the top.
  • Deflated Sharpe adjusts for the trials behind the run.
  • Every number links back to the exact methodology that produced it.
Overfitting, quantified

See the odds your edge was an accident.

Search hard enough and the past always offers a strategy that looks brilliant. The PBO panel puts a number on that danger, and the DSR panel beside it tells you whether the Sharpe survives once the size of your search is priced in. Together they turn "looks good" into a probability you can defend.

  • Probability of backtest overfitting across your full trial set.
  • Deflated Sharpe that accounts for selection, length, and skew.
  • Read it before you commit capital, not after a drawdown explains it.
