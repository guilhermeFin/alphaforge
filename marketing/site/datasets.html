Point-in-time

Why point-in-time matters

Three quiet ways ordinary financial data leaks the future into your backtest — and what point-in-time discipline does about each.

▦

Filing date vs. fiscal period

A quarter ends in March, but the numbers describing it may not hit the wire until May. Index fundamentals by the period they cover and your strategy is trading on figures nobody could read yet — classic look-ahead. PIT keys every value to when it was actually published.

⟲

Restatement vintages

Companies revise earnings, and most databases overwrite the old figure with the corrected one. Backtest on that and you're acting on a number the market never saw on the day. True PIT preserves each vintage, so the test reads what was known then — not what was fixed later.

◐

Survivorship bias

A universe built from today's listed names has quietly deleted everything that went bankrupt, merged away, or delisted. Test inside it and you've already excluded the losers — inflating returns for free. Honest data keeps the casualties in the pool.
